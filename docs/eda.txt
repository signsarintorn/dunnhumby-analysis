# Exploratory data analysis

Scope: merchandise only, from `sql/02_eda.sql`. Numbers in sections 1 and 2 include all sales
(merchandise and non-merchandise), as they were run before the non-merchandise flag was built.

## 1. Weekly trend

1. Weeks 1 to 16 show households still joining the panel, so sales climb from near zero.
   They are excluded from all analysis.
2. Week 102 covers only 6 days (days 706 to 711). Its sales per day (11,995) match earlier weeks,
   so the lower total reflects the missing day. It is kept for customer-level analysis and
   excluded from week-to-week comparisons.
3. From week 17 onward, weekly sales vary little: half of all weeks fall between 83,011 and 90,463
   (cv 7.7%), and the number of shopping households varies even less (cv 3.0%).

## 2. Sales drivers and 17-week blocks

Weekly sales = shopping households x trips per household x spend per basket.

| Weeks | Avg weekly sales | Households | Trips per household | Spend per basket |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 to 33 | 81,016 | 1,304 | 2.27 | 27.37 |
| 34 to 50 | 86,186 | 1,301 | 2.23 | 29.65 |
| 51 to 67 | 87,675 | 1,331 | 2.26 | 29.20 |
| 68 to 84 | 87,091 | 1,339 | 2.24 | 29.01 |
| 85 to 101 | 92,453 | 1,341 | 2.17 | 31.71 |

1. In most of the top and bottom 10 weeks, spend per basket moved more than households or trips.
2. Sales rise in weeks 85 to 101 with bigger baskets and slightly fewer trips. This first looked
   like growth. Q3 showed it is the holiday season, and weeks 34 to 50 hold the first-year
   holiday season. Regular weeks 50 to 84 are therefore used as the baseline.

## 3. Product and department mix

1. 7 of 42 departments hold about 93% of sales. GROCERY alone holds about 51%.
2. Non-merchandise (fuel, coupons, misc transactions, postage, donations) is about 8 to 10% of
   sales but only about 1.2% of line items. Fuel is worth about 10 times more per line item than
   groceries (24.92 vs 2.49).
3. 668 products are flagged as non-merchandise in the view `product_clean`.
4. Non-merchandise does not explain the late rise in spend per basket. Its share of sales fell
   (9.8% to 8.0%) while spend per basket for merchandise rose 10.6% (29.05 to 32.13).

## 4. Shopping frequency

Baskets with merchandise per household, weeks 17 to 102:

| p10 | p25 | Median | p75 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 32 | 67 | 120 | 193 |

1. The median household shops about every 9 days. The lightest 10% shop about every 40 days and
   the heaviest 10% about every 3 days.
2. About 9.2% of baskets contain only non-merchandise (for example a fuel-only stop). These are
   not counted as shopping trips.
3. Frequency is very uneven, so RFM scores use quintiles, and churn risk is judged against each
   household's own rhythm.

## 5. Campaigns and coupons

1. 30 campaigns of three types. TypeA (5 campaigns) reach 13 to 45% of households each and
   1,513 households in total. TypeB (19) and TypeC (6) are small, often under 20 households.
2. No campaign runs in weeks 17 to 32. From week 33, campaigns overlap continuously, so campaign
   weeks cannot be compared with non-campaign weeks.
3. 434 households (17.4%) ever redeemed a coupon, 5.3 times each on average. Redemption rates
   among targeted households: TypeA 25.3%, TypeB 13.8%, TypeC 9.6%.
4. Every redemption comes from a household listed as targeted, so `campaign_table` is a complete
   list of targeted households.
5. Targeting is not random. In weeks 17 to 32, households that later received TypeA had a median
   spend of 516 vs 88 for the rest and made 18 vs 4 trips. Only 1.3% made no purchase,
   vs 13.1% of the others.
