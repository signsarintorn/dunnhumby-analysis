# Methods

## Scope and cleaning

1. Merchandise only. Non-merchandise products are flagged in `product_clean` by department
   (fuel, misc transactions, coupons, postage, donations), by commodity (coupon and misc items,
   no description) or by sub-commodity containing "gasoline".
2. `transactions_clean` keeps rows with sales and quantity above zero. It is used for revenue,
   RFM and campaign analysis.
3. `transactions_basket` also keeps 3,748 zero-sales rows that carry a store discount (promotional
   items sold at no charge). It is used for basket analysis.
4. Weeks 17 to 101 are used for week-to-week comparisons. Customer-level analysis uses weeks
   17 to 102. Week number equals (day + 8) / 7 (integer division) for every row.
5. Regular weeks 50 to 84 form the baseline. Weeks 17 to 32 are still settling, and weeks 33 to 49
   and 85 to 101 are holiday seasons.

## Q1 RFM segmentation

1. Recency: days between a household's last merchandise trip and the last day of the data (711).
   Frequency: number of baskets with at least one merchandise item. Monetary: total merchandise spend.
2. Each measure is scored 1 to 5 using cut-offs at the 20th, 40th, 60th and 80th percentiles,
   so households with the same value always get the same score. Recency is reversed so 5 is best.
3. Frequency and Monetary scores are averaged into FM, since 91% of households score within one
   point on both (correlation 0.80).
4. Segments: Champions (R 4 to 5, FM 4 to 5), Loyal (R 3, FM 4 to 5), Potential (R 3 to 5,
   FM 2 to 3), Light (R 3 to 5, FM 1), At risk (R 1 to 2, FM 3 to 5), Lapsed (the rest).
5. Usual gap: time between first and last trip divided by trips minus one. A median version uses
   the middle value of all gaps between trip days and is less affected by one long absence.
6. Rhythm status compares days since the last trip with the usual gap: On schedule (up to 1 gap),
   Slightly late (up to 2), Late (up to 3), Overdue (more than 3). Households with one trip are Unknown.

## Q2 Campaign lift

1. Sales drivers: weekly sales are split into households, trips per household and spend per basket.
   Taking logs turns the product into a sum, and each part's share of the variation in log sales
   is its covariance with log sales divided by the variance of log sales. Shares add to 100%.
2. Lift (difference-in-differences): for each household, weekly spend during the campaign minus
   weekly spend in a baseline period. Households are grouped into deciles by baseline spend.
   Lift is the average change of targeted households minus the average change of non-targeted
   households in the same decile, weighted by targeted households.
3. Two baselines were used for campaign 18 (weeks 85 to 92): weeks 17 to 32 (before any campaign,
   but a year old) and weeks 77 to 84 (recent, but overlapping other campaigns).
4. Pull-forward check: the same comparison for the 8 weeks after the campaign (weeks 93 to 100),
   with and without households that received later campaigns.
5. Placebo test: the same method applied to two periods with no campaigns (weeks 17 to 24 vs
   25 to 32). The result should be zero if targeted and comparison households would otherwise
   change in the same way.

## Q3 Basket analysis

1. Spend per basket drivers: for each commodity, sales divided by the number of baskets in each
   period (weeks 68 to 84 and 85 to 101). The change per commodity, divided by the total change,
   gives its share of the rise.
2. Product pairs at commodity level. Support: share of all baskets containing both. Confidence:
   share of baskets with one item that also contain the other. Lift: observed baskets with both
   divided by the number expected if the items were unrelated.
3. Only pairs found together in at least 500 baskets (100 in the holiday period) are kept.
4. Pair types: Cross-sell candidate (lift 4 or more and support 1% or more), Strong but niche
   (lift 4 or more, support under 1%), Loosely linked (lift 2 to 4).

## Dashboard

1. Summary tables in `sql/06_dashboard_tables.sql` are loaded into Power BI in Import mode.
2. Baseline measures remove all filters and use regular weeks 50 to 84, so every bar is compared
   with the same reference.
3. "Vs a regular week" charts show each week's value divided by the regular-week average, minus one.
